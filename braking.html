The problem

Every car brakes the same way: squeeze a heavy disc and turn speed into heat. Iron is cheap but heavy. Carbon-ceramic is light but costs many times more. Racing pays for lightness. Everyone else carries the weight.

How it works

00

One disc, two parts.

A wave you brake on, and plates around it that hold it and cool it.

01

The pad rubs only the wave.

The shiny zigzag is the braking band, full thickness. The dark plates sit lower, so the pad never touches them.

02

The contact sweeps in and out.

As the disc turns, the wave slides back and forth under the pad. Heat and wear spread over the whole pad face instead of one stripe.

03

The plates pull the heat out.

Braking heats the wave. The finned plates around it carry that heat into the air.

04

The wave flexes. The disc stays true.

A wave can grow with heat without warping or cracking. That is how 10 mm of steel does the job of 32 mm of iron.
